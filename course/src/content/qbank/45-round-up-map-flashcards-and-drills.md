## 1. The loop, for this track

1. **Gather (fast pass).** Go through lessons 1 to 44 quickly: read the goals and the recap, run the commands, skim the rest. Use the **notes box** at the top of each lesson to dump what you learn and what confuses you.
2. **Refine.** Turn the notes into a map and cards (section 2 and section 3). Draw the map from memory first, then compare.
3. **Drill.** Work the flashcards (section 3), then the **Your turn** tasks and quizzes in each lesson.
4. **Round 2.** Run it again only on what is left (section 6): long scenarios, new tools, edge cases, odd details.

Open the whole toolkit any time from the **Study** button at the top: [Method](#study/method), [Drill](#study/drill), [Maps](#study/maps), [Notebook](#study/notebook).

## 2. The map: One answer shape for every interview question

Whatever the topic, a strong answer has the same four beats. Use the topic lessons as the raw material.

1. **Say it simply**: One everyday picture so the interviewer hears you understand the idea, not just the words. Lessons: [1](#qbank-1).
2. **Name the parts**: List the components or steps in order. For scenario questions, list the request path or the triage order. Lessons: [2](#qbank-2), [3](#qbank-3), [9](#qbank-9).
3. **Show the evidence**: Name the exact command or metric you would check first, and what a bad result looks like. Lessons: [12](#qbank-12), [13](#qbank-13), [37](#qbank-37).
4. **Give the fix and the trade-off**: A fix without a trade-off sounds junior. Say what it costs and when you would not use it. Lessons: [19](#qbank-19), [20](#qbank-20), [25](#qbank-25), [28](#qbank-28).
5. **Close with the sentence**: Finish with the two or three sentences from 'What to say to the interviewer', in your own words. Lessons: [34](#qbank-34), [40](#qbank-40), [42](#qbank-42).

**Do this now:** [open the sketch pad for this track](#study/maps/qbank), draw the path from memory, then reveal the map and fix what you missed. Paper or an iPad canvas works just as well. The map is a scaffold for recall: every topic in this track should hang from one of these hops.

## 4. Tips nobody tells you

- Cover the answer, say yours out loud, then compare. Reading is not remembering.
- Do Basic first for your stack, then Scenario-based: most interviews are troubleshooting.
- Prepare two real stories (an incident, a migration) from your own work; the behavioural lessons show the shape.
- If you do not know, say how you would find out: the command, the doc page, who you would ask.
- Run the examples in the Lab or a free real machine; hands-on answers sound different.

## 6. Round 2: make these boring

- Redo only the cards you missed, then answer the Scenario lessons for your stack without looking.
- Pick five questions at random and answer each in 60 seconds, out loud, then compare.
- Pair a scenario question with a real incident from your own history and tell them as one story.
